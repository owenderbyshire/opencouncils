(i)      Question from D. Hill

Will the Cabinet Member for Education and the Director of Learning and Skills
address the systemic disadvantage faced by rural families due to the lack of
alignment between primary feeder school networks and secondary school catchment
boundaries?

Currently, rural families who send their children to their designated local primary
schools find themselves excluded from free secondary transport because the
Council’s rigid 'nearest school' policy does not match historical or educational
catchments. This forces a situation where some pupils receive fully funded
transport, while neighbouring rural families are refused even the option to purchase
commercial spare seats – despite the Department for Transport extending the


TRIM – Council 2026
October 05 Minutes (JLT)
                                           277


PSVAR exemptions for home-to-school transport specifically to protect rural
capacity.

What urgent steps is this Council taking to review its learner travel policy so that rural
working families are not unfairly penalised by outdated, non-aligned catchment
boundaries and restricted access to purchase passes?

Reply from the Cabinet Member for Education, Arts and the Welsh Language

The Council's Home to School Transport Policy is based on the requirements of the
Learner Travel (Wales) Measure 2008 and applies consistently to all learners.
Eligibility for free transport is determined by a learner's home address and their
catchment school, rather than the primary school they previously attended.

Parents may choose a school outside their designated catchment arrangements;
however, this does not create an entitlement to free home to school transport.

The Council reviews school admissions arrangements on an annual basis.

The Council may offer spare seats on school transport services where capacity is
available. The current cost of a spare seat is £470 per academic year. Pupils who
purchase a spare seat are not eligible for free school transport because they either
live within the statutory walking distance or attend a school other than their
designated catchment school.

The Department for Transport (DfT) has long required school transport services to
comply with the Public Service Vehicle Accessibility Regulations (PSVAR). Under
these regulations, any vehicle with a capacity exceeding 22 passengers must
provide:

•        Wheelchair spaces
•        Boarding lifts or ramps
•        Handrails and step markings
•        Accessible seating.

The majority of vehicles used for school transport services with a capacity of more
than 22 passengers are coaches that do not meet PSVAR requirements.

An exemption previously granted by the DfT, which allowed operators to continue
using non-compliant vehicles and enabled Councils to sell spare seats, expired on
31st July 2026. Prior to the end of the academic year, the Council wrote to all families
who had previously purchased spare seat passes to advise them that, in the
absence of a further exemption from the DfT, spare seat sales would no longer be
possible.

However, in late July 2026, the Council was informed that the DfT had reversed its
decision and would once again allow operators to apply for an exemption. This
exemption would permit the continued sale of spare seats on eligible school
transport services. The Council therefore asked all operators to submit applications
and confirm when an exemption had been granted. It should be noted that

TRIM – Council 2026
October 05 Minutes (JLT)
                                          278


participation in this process was voluntary, as operators were under no obligation to
apply.

The majority of operators have now successfully obtained the exemption and, as of
21st September 2026, the spare seat purchase system has reopened. However,
where an operator has either not applied for, or has not been granted, an exemption,
the Council is unable to sell spare seats on those services.

(ii)     Question from D. Weston

The public footpath is covered in a large number of tension cracks. Probably, more
per meter than there is without. There are also several areas where temporary heras
fencing has been erected, presumably due to poor and unstable ground conditions.

Could the Cabinet please advise on the current plan to correct the defects and
attend to the overgrowth? The situation has been ongoing for some time with little or
no sign of the ground of works taking place that would provide a long term solution to
the problem. Could the Cabinet advise on a timescale when physical works will
commence and an anticipated completion date?

Reply from the Cabinet Member for Neighbourhood and Building Services

Thank you for your question, Mr. Weston.

Firstly, it is important to distinguish between the pathways within the White Farm
open space and the primary public pathway adjacent to the development, as
responsibility for these areas differs.

The primary public pathway, where temporary Heras fencing has been erected, has
not yet been adopted by the Council and remains the responsibility of the developer.
The Council would not be prepared to adopt the pathway in its current condition
given the extent of the defects, including tension cracking and evidence of ground
instability. Officers continue to engage with the developer regarding the
investigations and remedial works required before the pathway can be considered
for adoption.

A number of tension cracks have also been identified on pathways within the
adopted White Farm open space, which are maintained by the Council. Officers are
currently assessing the most appropriate engineering solution to ensure that any
works provide a durable, long-term remedy rather than simply addressing the visible
defects.

The Council is also aware of concerns regarding overgrowth and will continue to
undertake maintenance where required. Temporary safety measures will remain in
place whilst investigations and assessments are completed.

Whilst I appreciate residents' frustration, it is important that any solution addresses
the underlying causes rather than providing a short-term fix. Consequently, a
definitive start and completion date cannot yet be provided. Works to the unadopted
pathway remain a matter for the developer, whilst the Council is actively progressing

preferred remedial approach and programme of works have been established. In the
meantime, the area will continue to be monitored to ensure public safety.

What action is the Council taking to reduce the flood and ice risks posed by blocked
drains across the Vale of Glamorgan?

Reply from the Cabinet Member for Neighbourhood and Building Services

The Council recognises the importance of keeping our highway drainage network
working effectively, particularly in reducing the risks of flooding, standing water and
ice.

We have a programme of gully cleansing and drainage maintenance across the
Vale, alongside targeted inspections and additional work at locations with a known
history of flooding or drainage problems.

We also investigate reports from residents, Elected Members and our officers. These
are prioritised according to risk, so that locations presenting the greatest potential
danger to highway users receive the appropriate attention.

Severe and prolonged weather inevitably places additional pressure on the drainage
network. That is why we combine planned preventative maintenance with a risk-
based response to emerging problems.

Our priority is straightforward: to use the resources available as effectively as
possible, tackle the locations presenting the greatest risk, and help keep the Vale’s
roads safe and resilient.

The Council recognises the importance of maintaining the highway drainage network
to minimise the risk of flooding and ice across the Vale of Glamorgan.

As with many highway authorities, the Council must balance planned maintenance
with increasing demands arising from severe weather events. Consequently, a risk-
based approach is adopted, prioritising blocked gullies, flooding incidents and other
safety-critical issues to ensure available resources deliver the greatest benefit to
residents and highway users.

How many contractors does the Council currently engage to carry out highway and

Reply from the Cabinet Member for Neighbourhood and Building Services

The Council currently engages a single principal contractor, Centregreat Ltd, to
undertake highway and footway repair works across the Vale of Glamorgan. This
arrangement provides the Council with an established delivery mechanism for
undertaking planned and responsive maintenance across the highway network,
including carriageway and footway repairs.

Centregreat Ltd was appointed through the South East Wales Highway Framework.
The framework provides participating local authorities with a structured and
compliant procurement route through which suitably qualified contractors can be
appointed to undertake highway-related works.

The use of the framework enables the Council to secure competitive and
demonstrable value for money while also ensuring that contractors have already
demonstrated the necessary technical competence, experience, financial capacity
and ability to meet the relevant health and safety and industry requirements.

The framework approach also assists the Council by reducing the time and
administrative costs that would otherwise be associated with undertaking separate
procurement exercises for individual programmes of highway works.
